A local-first bug recorder

Most bug-capture tools upload every recording to the vendor’s cloud. Session Recorder stores recordings in your browser, and anything that leaves goes to an account you configured, not to us.

Session Recorder is a free, open-source Chrome extension for recording browser bugs. It has no account system, no developer-operated backend and no telemetry. Sessions are written to IndexedDB in your Chrome profile.

What “local-first” means here

  • No account. Install it and press Record. There is no sign-up, workspace or seat.
  • No backend of ours. The extension makes no requests to any server operated by its author.
  • No telemetry. No analytics, crash reporting or usage counts.
  • Storage is your browser. Deleting a session deletes the local data.
  • Export is a file. A ZIP in your Downloads folder, shared however you choose.
  • No remote code. Everything the extension runs ships in the package.

Optional integrations, on your accounts

Four features send data out, and only after you configure them with your own credentials. Each sends directly from the extension to that service:

  • Transcription sends recorded audio to Deepgram, OpenAI or ElevenLabs.
  • AI summaries and session names send up to 60,000 characters of text evidence, never media, to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or your compatible endpoint.
  • Publishing uploads the report and its media to your Cloudflare Pages project, behind your Cloudflare Access policy.
  • GitHub issues send the title, summary and report link to the repository you choose.

Spoken annotation comments use the same transcription provider. With an AI model configured, selecting an annotation also sends a bounded excerpt of visible page labels (never input values or hidden content) to the model to build a short vocabulary list for Deepgram or ElevenLabs; neither is saved. Provider pricing, limits and retention policies apply to all of these. The privacy policy lists them in full.

Secrets are masked before they are written

Redaction runs at capture time. Authorization headers, cookies, token-like JSON and form fields, sensitive URL parameters and password inputs are masked on the way into storage, so the unmasked value is never saved. You can add rules or turn redaction off per session.

It is not a guarantee. Redaction is pattern-based and does not inspect screenshots, video or audio, or recognise personal data in ordinary fields. Review evidence before sharing it. Details and limits.

Who this design suits

  • Internal and regulated apps, where recordings of production screens should not land in a third-party workspace.
  • Pre-release work, where a recording of an unannounced feature should stay with the team.
  • Teams already on GitHub and Cloudflare, who would rather use those accounts than add another vendor.
  • Agent workflows with Claude Code, Cursor or Codex against a local checkout.

The trade-offs

You give upWhat to do instead
A shared vendor workspacePublish protected reports to your Cloudflare account and discuss them in GitHub issues.
Integrations beyond GitHubUse Publish report only for a link, or attach the ZIP to any tracker.
Cross-device historySessions live in one Chrome profile. Export or publish what others need.
Reports from customersUse a widget-based tool. See the comparisons.
