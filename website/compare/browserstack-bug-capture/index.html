Session Recorder vs BrowserStack Bug Capture

Both help a developer see a bug they did not witness. They make different assumptions about who reports, where reports live and what you pay for. Prices and plans were checked on October 25, 2026.

The short answer

Choose BrowserStack Bug Capture if:

  • Your team already pays for BrowserStack and testing across real browsers and devices.
  • You file into Jira, Linear or Slack as well as GitHub.
  • You want instant replay and a hosted place to share captures.

Choose Session Recorder if:

  • You do not use BrowserStack and want a free, standalone recorder.
  • You want reports stored in your own Cloudflare account, behind your own Access policy.
  • Annotations should drive an AI summary from the model you choose.

About BrowserStack Bug Capture

BrowserStack Bug Capture, previously Bird Eats Bug, records browser bugs with video, clicks, console and network logs and instant replay, and sends them to GitHub, Jira, Linear, Slack and other tools. It is now part of BrowserStack and is offered with BrowserStack plans. Bug Capture

Pricing

Bug Capture is bundled with eligible BrowserStack plans rather than sold at a separate list price we could verify. Legacy Bird Eats Bug pricing pages no longer match the current offer. Check BrowserStack’s pricing page for which package includes it. browserstack.com/pricing, checked October 25, 2026 Bird Eats Bug pricing

Session Recorder is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0, with no plan tiers. Optional integrations use your own accounts: model providers and transcription services bill usage, and published reports use Cloudflare Pages Functions, where your Cloudflare plan’s limits and billing apply.

How the workflows differ

Hosted workspace or your own accounts. Hosted tools give everyone one place to view, comment on and route reports, with no setup beyond inviting people. Session Recorder has no workspace. Reports live in the browser that recorded them, and when you share them they go to your own Cloudflare Pages project and GitHub repository. That means a one-time setup and fewer collaboration features, in exchange for keeping the data under accounts you already control.

Who is reporting. Widget-based tools let clients and customers report without installing anything. Session Recorder assumes the reporter can install a Chrome extension, which fits internal QA and product teams.

What the developer gets. Session Recorder puts annotations first, then the full timeline of interactions, console output and network requests with bodies. Exports are Markdown designed for coding agents as well as people.

Cost model. Paid tools charge per member, creator or plan tier. Session Recorder costs nothing, but optional AI summaries, transcription and publishing run on your provider accounts, where usage charges and limits apply.

What Session Recorder does not do

Session Recorder does not offer a hosted shared workspace, client review boards, surveys, broad native integrations beyond GitHub, or enterprise administration such as SSO and seat management. If you need those, a hosted product is the better choice.
